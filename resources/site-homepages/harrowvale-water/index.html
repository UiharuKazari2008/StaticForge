Under District Ordinance 94-12, every testable backflow prevention assembly on a commercial, irrigation or fire service connection must be tested annually by a District-certified tester. Test reports are due within 10 business days of the test date. Assemblies not tested by the due date shown on your notice may result in suspension of water service after a 30-day final notice.

What is a cross-connection?

A cross-connection is any actual or possible connection between the drinking water system and a pipe, tank, fixture or piece of equipment that could hold something other than drinking water. Water normally flows one way, from the District main to your building. Backflow is water flowing the wrong way, and it happens in two ways:

  • Backpressure - pressure on the customer's side becomes higher than the supply pressure, for instance from a pump, a boiler or an elevated tank.
  • Backsiphonage - supply pressure drops, for instance during a main break or heavy hydrant use, and water is drawn back out of the customer's piping.

Hazard classification

The District classifies each connection when a service is set up and again when the use of a property changes.

  • High hazard (health hazard) - anything that could cause illness or death if it reached the drinking water: chemicals, sewage, laboratory and medical equipment, irrigation with fertilizer or chemical injection, and fire sprinkler systems containing antifreeze or connected to a non-drinking water source.
  • Low hazard (non-health hazard) - substances that would make water look, taste or smell objectionable but are not a danger to health, such as water standing in an ordinary wet-pipe fire sprinkler system.

Accepted assembly types

AssemblyProtects againstUse
Reduced pressure principle assembly (RP)Backpressure and backsiphonageHigh or low hazard. Two independent check valves with a relief valve between them that opens to the atmosphere. Must be installed above grade, never in a pit, with a drain able to carry the relief valve discharge.
Double check valve assembly (DC)Backpressure and backsiphonageLow hazard only.
Pressure vacuum breaker (PVB)Backsiphonage onlyHigh or low hazard where there is no possibility of backpressure, mostly irrigation. Must be at least 12 inches above the highest downstream outlet or piping. May discharge water when it opens, so it is installed outdoors or over a drain.
Spill-resistant vacuum breaker (SVB)Backsiphonage onlyAs for the PVB, but designed not to spill when it pressurizes, so it may be used indoors.
Reduced pressure detector assembly (RPDA) and double check detector assembly (DCDA)As RP and DCFire services. A small metered bypass assembly shows any water use or leakage on the fire line.

An air gap (a physical vertical separation between the end of a supply pipe and the flood rim of the receiving tank) is also accepted for any hazard, but it must be inspected by the District. Atmospheric vacuum breakers built into fixtures are not testable and do not satisfy the annual testing requirement.

Annual testing - what to expect

  1. About 30 days before your due date the District mails a test notice to the account holder, listing each assembly by serial number.
  2. Hire a tester from the list of District-certified testers. The District does not test customer-owned assemblies.
  3. The tester completes Form BF-1 for each assembly and sends it to the District within 10 business days of the test.
  4. An assembly that fails must be repaired or replaced and retested within 30 days. Replacements also need Form BF-2.
  5. If no passing report is received, a 30-day final notice is mailed. Service may then be suspended until a passing report is received.

Installing an assembly on a service line creates a closed system. Water heaters and boilers in a closed system need room for thermal expansion; talk to your plumber about an expansion tank before the assembly is installed.
