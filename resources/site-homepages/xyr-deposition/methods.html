Field and laboratory methods

This page summarises the methods in use since the current collector was installed in December 2016. The full station operating procedure (Environmental Monitoring Section procedure EMS-WD-03, revision 7) is available on request. Changes to equipment and methods are recorded in the station log.

1. Site and equipment

  • Wet-only collector. A single high-density polyethylene bucket (inner diameter 29.0 cm, collecting area 660.5 cm2, capacity 13 L) under a motor-driven lid. The lid closes over the bucket in dry weather to exclude dry deposition.
  • Precipitation sensor. A heated grid sensor mounted beside the collector. When the grid is wetted the lid opens within a few seconds; it closes about two minutes after the grid has dried. Heating dries the grid after rain ends and melts snow that settles on it. Lid openings are recorded by the station logger.
  • Precipitation gauge. A weighing gauge (orifice 200 cm2, resolution 0.1 mm) about 5 m from the collector provides the reference precipitation depth for each sample period. The gauge logger has its own battery supply.
  • Weather data. Wind direction, wind speed and air temperature for comparison are taken from the hourly records of the District 21 meteorological observatory.

2. Sample collection

The bucket is changed every Tuesday between 09:00 and 11:00. A clean bucket, rinsed with deionised water in the laboratory and sealed in a polyethylene bag, is fitted and the exposed bucket is capped and placed in an insulated box with cold packs. The sample ID is assigned from the year and period number of the change date: the period ending on the first Tuesday of the year is period 001. If no precipitation fell during the week the lid stays closed and the period is recorded as dry; the bucket is still exchanged. Winter samples containing snow are left to melt at room temperature in the sealed bucket on arrival at the laboratory.

3. Sample handling and storage

  • The sample is weighed in the bucket to determine its volume (density taken as 1.00 g/mL). In 2025 the mean collection efficiency, sample volume divided by the volume expected from the gauge depth, was 95%.
  • Unfiltered aliquots are used for pH and conductivity, measured on the day of receipt where possible and always within 48 hours.
  • An aliquot for ion chromatography is filtered (0.45 µm) into a pre-rinsed bottle and stored in the dark at 4 °C. Analysis is completed within 14 days of sample change. The cold room temperature is logged every 15 minutes.
  • Remaining sample is kept at 4 °C for six months for reanalysis and then discarded.

Calibration standards are prepared from certified stock solutions. A check standard is analysed after every ten samples and must recover within 5% of its nominal value; one sample in ten is analysed in duplicate. Concentrations are reported in µeq/L. The H+ concentration is calculated from pH as [H+] = 10(6 - pH) µeq/L.

5. Geothermal sector screening

The District 21 geothermal power station lies on a peak about 6 km west-south-west of the station. Hydrogen sulfide emitted by geothermal plants oxidises in the atmosphere to sulfate, so precipitation that arrives on winds from that direction can carry a local sulfur contribution that is not representative of the reservoir catchment as a whole. For each sample period the hours with the collector lid open are matched with the observatory's hourly wind direction. If wind was from the geothermal sector (225° to 270°) for 50% or more of those hours, the sample is flagged GEO and excluded from the annual summaries. GEO samples are analysed and archived in the normal way. The rule was introduced in January 2023 and applied to all samples from 2017 onward.

6. Calculations

Precipitation-weighted mean (PWM). For valid samples i with concentration Ci and gauge precipitation Pi for the same period:

PWM = ∑(Ci x Pi) / ∑Pi

The gauge depth is used for weighting rather than the sample volume, so that weighting is not affected by small differences in collection efficiency. The annual pH is calculated from the PWM of H+, not by averaging pH values.

Non-sea-salt sulfate. nss-SO42- = SO42- - 0.121 x Na+ (all in µeq/L), using the sulfate to sodium equivalent ratio of sea water and assuming all sodium is of marine origin.

Wet deposition. Annual deposition D of an ion is the PWM concentration multiplied by the total gauge precipitation P for all periods in the year, including periods without a valid sample:

D (kg/ha) = PWM (µeq/L) x E (mg/meq) x P (mm) x 10-5

where E is the equivalent weight of the ion. The factor follows from 1 mm of precipitation on 1 m2 being 1 L, and 1 kg/ha being 100 mg/m2. Equivalent weights used (mg/meq): H+ 1.008, SO42- 48.03, NO3- 62.00, Cl- 35.45, NH4+ 18.04, Na+ 22.99, K+ 39.10, Ca2+ 20.04, Mg2+ 12.15. Inorganic nitrogen uses 14.007 mg N/meq for both NO3- and NH4+.

Example, sulfate 2025: 17.1 µeq/L x 48.03 mg/meq x 1765.0 mm x 10-5 = 14.50 kg/ha.
