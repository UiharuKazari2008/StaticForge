Quality assurance and quality control

Each sample is checked in the field, at receipt in the laboratory and after analysis. A sample is valid when it has a complete analysis, no field or laboratory problem that affects its composition, and passes the ion balance and conductivity checks below. Invalid samples are retained in the archive with a flag but are excluded from all summaries on this site.

Ion balance

The percent difference between the cation and anion sums is calculated for each sample:

PD = 100 x (∑cations - ∑anions) / (∑cations + ∑anions)

with cations = H+ + NH4+ + Na+ + K+ + Ca2+ + Mg2+ and anions = SO42- + NO3- + Cl-, all in µeq/L. Bicarbonate is not measured; in equilibrium with atmospheric CO2 it is below about 2 µeq/L at pH 5.5 and much less in more acidic samples, so it is not included. Acceptance limits depend on the total ion sum:

Total ion sum (µeq/L)Acceptable PD
less than 50±25%
50 to less than 100±15%
100 or more±10%

Samples outside the limits are reanalysed. If the reanalysis confirms the result, the sample is flagged and excluded. A small positive median PD (an anion deficit) is normal at this site and is attributed mainly to weak organic acids, which are not measured, and to bicarbonate in the least acidic samples.

Conductivity check. Conductivity is also calculated from the ion concentrations using limiting equivalent conductances at 25 °C (S cm2/eq: H+ 349.8, NH4+ 73.5, Na+ 50.1, K+ 73.5, ½Ca2+ 59.5, ½Mg2+ 53.0, ½SO42- 80.0, NO3- 71.4, Cl- 76.3). Measured and calculated conductivity must agree within 15%.

Geothermal sector screening

Samples from weeks in which the wind, during the hours the lid was open, came from the geothermal station sector (225° to 270°) for 50% or more of the time are flagged GEO and excluded from the summaries, because hydrogen sulfide from the geothermal plant oxidises to sulfate and adds a local contribution. Wind data are from the District 21 meteorological observatory. See Methods, section 5. GEO samples are included in the ion balance counts above, since their analyses are complete. Flagged by year: 2017: 1, 2018: 2, 2019: 2, 2020: 3, 2021: 1, 2022: 0, 2023: 0, 2024: 0, 2025: 3; none so far in 2026.

Completeness criteria

An annual summary meets the completeness criteria when both of the following hold:

  • valid samples account for at least 75% of the precipitation recorded by the gauge for the year (precipitation coverage), and
  • at least 75% of the sample periods in the year are either valid or dry.

The gauge record has been complete for every year shown. Summaries that do not meet the criteria are published but marked. 2021 does not meet the first criterion because the collector was without power from late July to early October 2021 (see the station log).
