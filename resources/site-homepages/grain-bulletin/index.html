Quarterly Bulletin No. 117: Grain Moisture Meter Calibration Results, Second Quarter 2026

Release date: Friday 14 August 2026, 09:30 | Reference period: 1 April to 30 June 2026 | Next release: Friday 13 November 2026 (Q3 2026) | Classification: Official statistics

This bulletin reports the results of calibration checks carried out by Service inspectors on in-service grain moisture meters at licensed receiving points during the quarter. Each check compares a meter's readings with reference moisture values on prepared samples of one grain type. Results are not seasonally adjusted. The mix of grain types checked follows the receiving season, so all-grain figures for different quarters should be compared with care.

Main points

  • 1,195 checks were made on 731 meters at 417 licensed receiving points.
  • 1,113 checks (93.1%) were within the maintenance tolerance of plus or minus 0.5 percentage points, compared with 94.1% in Q1 2026 (revised) and 92.4% in Q2 2025.
  • The mean error across all checks was +0.04 pp, with a standard deviation of 0.28 pp.
  • Oats (88.0%) and malting barley (88.6%) had the lowest proportions within tolerance. Meters read high on average for both.
  • Of the 82 checks outside tolerance, 67 were resolved on the day by adjustment and re-check. In the other 15 the meter was withdrawn from service for that grain type pending repair.

Revisions

Figures for Q1 2026 have been revised to include returns from four receiving points that arrived after the cut-off for Bulletin No. 116. The number of checks rose from 975 to 994 and the number within tolerance from 917 to 935. The proportion within tolerance is unchanged at 94.1%. Revised figures are marked (r). See the revisions policy.

Notes

  1. A check is one meter tested on one grain type against three reference samples. A meter tested on more than one grain type contributes one check for each.
  2. pp = percentage points of moisture content, wet basis. Mean error is the meter reading minus the reference value, so a positive figure means meters read high on average.
  3. A check is within tolerance when the mean of its three sample errors is within plus or minus 0.5 pp (Service Order MS-14, Schedule 2).
  4. Reference moisture values were determined by the air-oven method at the Academy City Research automated grain laboratory in School District 17, under contract to the Service.
  5. Meters outside tolerance were adjusted and re-checked, or withdrawn from service for that grain type, on the day of inspection. The tables count the first result of each check.
  6. Malting barley reference samples were prepared from the two-row spring cultivars Dreamscape and Marrowby (2025 harvest).
  7. (r) = revised.
