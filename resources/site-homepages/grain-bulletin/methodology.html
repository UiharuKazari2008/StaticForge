Methodology: in-service calibration checks

This note describes how the calibration checks reported in the quarterly bulletins are made and how the published statistics are derived. It applies from Bulletin No. 107 onwards.

1. Scope

The checks apply to grain moisture meters used at licensed receiving points to determine moisture content for the purchase, sale or storage of grain. Meters used only for on-farm management are outside scope. Six grain types are reported separately: hard red winter wheat, soft white wheat, malting barley, yellow dent corn, oats and sorghum.

2. Inspection programme

Every licensed receiving point is inspected at least once a year. Inspections are scheduled to fall before the main receiving season for the grain types each point handles, so the mix of grain types checked changes from quarter to quarter. Points where a meter was withdrawn or found outside tolerance at the previous inspection are revisited sooner. The checks are therefore not a random sample of meters in use, and the results describe the meters inspected in the quarter.

3. Reference samples

For each grain type, sets of three reference samples are prepared to span the low, middle and upper part of the moisture range normally received. Samples are cleaned, mixed, divided and sealed in moisture-proof containers. Before testing, sealed samples are left to reach room temperature, because meter readings depend on grain temperature and a cold sample opened in a warm room can gather condensation. Malting barley samples are prepared from the cultivars most widely delivered in the current season; for the 2025 harvest these were Dreamscape and Marrowby.

4. Reference moisture: the air-oven method

The reference moisture of every sample set is determined by the Academy City Research automated grain laboratory in School District 17, under contract to the Service. A weighed portion of the sample is dried in a forced-air oven at a set temperature for a set time and weighed again. The loss in mass is taken as the water driven off.

  • Wheat, barley and oats are ground before drying, and the ground portion is dried at 130 °C for one hour. Samples too moist to grind cleanly are first partly dried whole, and the two stages are combined in the result.
  • Corn is dried as whole kernels, at 103 °C for 72 hours.
  • The procedure sheet for each grain type, including sorghum, sets out the portion mass, grinding, oven temperature and drying time.

Each determination is made in duplicate on separate portions. If the duplicates do not agree within the laboratory's set limit, the determination is repeated. The reference value is the mean of the accepted duplicates.

5. Wet basis

All moisture figures are on a wet basis: the mass of water as a percentage of the mass of the grain including that water.

moisture (% wet basis) = (mass before drying - mass after drying) / mass before drying x 100

Errors and tolerances are differences between two such percentages and are given in percentage points (pp). For instance, a meter reading 14.6% on a sample with a reference value of 14.2% has an error of +0.4 pp.

6. The check

The inspector tests each of the three reference samples on the meter, using the meter's setting for that grain type and the manufacturer's operating procedure, and records the reading to 0.1%. Most meters in service estimate moisture from the electrical (dielectric) properties of the grain and correct the result for grain temperature. The error for each sample is the meter reading minus the reference value. The check error is the mean of the three sample errors.

7. Tolerance

A check is within tolerance when the check error is within plus or minus 0.5 pp. This is the maintenance tolerance for meters in service set by Service Order MS-14, Schedule 2. A meter outside tolerance is adjusted by the operator under the inspector's supervision and checked again, or withdrawn from service for that grain type until repaired. The bulletins count the first result of each check.

8. Published statistics

  • Checks: one meter tested on one grain type. A meter tested on two grain types counts as two checks.
  • Meters and receiving points: distinct meters and points with at least one check in the quarter.
  • % within: checks within tolerance as a percentage of checks, to one decimal place.
  • Mean error: the average of the check errors. A positive figure means meters read high on average.
  • Std. dev.: the sample standard deviation of the check errors, a measure of spread between meters.

Percentages, means and standard deviations are calculated from unrounded check records and then rounded, so they cannot always be reproduced exactly from the rounded figures in the tables. Counts in the tables add to their totals.

9. Quality and limitations

Reference values carry their own uncertainty, which the laboratory reports with each sample set; it is small compared with the tolerance. Results for grain types with few checks in a quarter, such as oats, can change noticeably from one quarter to the next. Results by meter model reflect where and on which grains each model happened to be checked and should not be read as a comparison of instruments.
